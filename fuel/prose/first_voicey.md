# Prose mode: first_voicey

First-person with a strong narrating personality. The teller is allowed to
look back, joke, digress, and arrange scenes for effect — but still stays
concrete and specific.

Use for comic novels, memoir-shaped fiction, picaresques, and anything where
the *voice* is the product (not live brain-eavesdropping).

## Required

- A consistent narrating self: diction, humor, obsessions, blind spots.
- Scene first; interiority in service of the scene, not a substitute for it.
- Past-tense default is fine; present works if the voice holds.
- Specific objects, jobs, rooms, and body facts over abstract mood.
- Dialogue that sounds like this narrator overheard it, not like stage directions.

## Forbidden

- Diary-summary emotion labels ("I was devastated").
- Staccato one-line beats used as drama ("I nod." / "I don't answer.").
- The same catchphrase or tic every chapter.
- Generic "so there I was" padding that could belong to any narrator.

## Distance

Medium-close. We're with the teller, not fused with their pre-verbal brain.
Thought can be reported in their idiom rather than rendered as raw syntax.
